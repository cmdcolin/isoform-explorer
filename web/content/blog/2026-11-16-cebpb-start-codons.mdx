C/EBPβ, encoded by _CEBPB_, is a transcription factor with a bZIP domain. The
gene has no introns, and its one mRNA is translated from three start codons in
the same reading frame
([Xiong et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/29.14.3087)). UniProt's
canonical protein, called LAP\* in the literature, starts at the first. LAP
starts at methionine {forms[1]?.first} and LIP at methionine{' '}
{forms[2]?.first}. The gene page sums up LIP, isoform {s.isoform.accession}:

> {s.answer}

## Three starts, one end

The drawing above puts the mRNA (RefSeq {CEBPB_MRNA.refseq}) and the three
proteins on one scale. All three end at the same stop codon, so they share
their last {forms[2]?.length} residues, and with them the bZIP domain
({bzip?.start}–{bzip?.end}), which binds DNA and pairs C/EBPβ with itself or
another C/EBP protein. LIP starts after the 9aaTAD activation motif
({tad?.start}–{tad?.end}) and the other N-terminal regions the gene page lists.

According to UniProt's note on LIP, which cites
[Zhu et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M108075200), LIP acts as a
dominant negative by forming heterodimers with LAP.

Between the first two start codons, RefSeq marks an upstream open reading frame
of 11 codons as regulatory, citing a study of how translation sets the balance
of the isoforms
([Calkhoven et al., 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10921906/)).

## One mRNA for all three proteins

The other posts check an isoform's splice junction in GTEx. No splice junction is
unique to LIP, and the three proteins come from one mRNA, so with RNA-seq you
can count that mRNA but not LIP's share of it. Ribosome profiling, which sequences
the stretches of mRNA under ribosomes, shows which start codons ribosomes use.

RefSeq lists LAP and LIP under two accessions, NM_001285878.1 and
NM_001285879.1, with the same {CEBPB_MRNA.length.toLocaleString()} bases and a
different coding sequence marked on each. GENCODE has no separate transcript
for either, so the gene page lists only the RefSeq ones.

Mass spectrometry has the same limit from the other side. LIP's sequence is the
last {forms[2]?.length} residues of LAP and LAP\*, so a peptide from inside it
matches all three, and only one that begins at LIP's first residue would tell
LIP apart.
