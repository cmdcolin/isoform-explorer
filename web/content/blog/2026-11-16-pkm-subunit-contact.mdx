Pyruvate kinase catalyses the last step of glycolysis. The _PKM_ gene encodes
two forms of it, M1 and M2, by including one exon or the other of a neighbouring
pair, a choice first traced in rat
([Noguchi et al., 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3020052/)). UniProt's
canonical sequence is M2, so M1 appears on the PKM page as isoform{' '}
{s.isoform.accession}, which the page sums up as:

> {s.answer}

## Mutually exclusive exons

M1's and M2's exons have the same length, so the reading frame holds either way, and
residues {swapStart}–{swapEnd} of M2 become as many different residues in M1.
Isoform explorer calls that a swap, with the stretch replaced and every other
residue present. The features in the replaced stretch are listed all the same, and one
of them is part of the binding site for fructose 1,6-bisphosphate (FBP), M2's
allosteric activator.

According to UniProt's note on M1, it is active by itself, with no need for
FBP, and it replaces M2 in adult tissues after birth.

## Where the stretch sits

The drawing above is the crystal structure of M2 bound to FBP
([PDB 3BJF](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/3BJF),
[Christofk et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06667)), four subunits in
four pale colours, with residues {swapStart}–{swapEnd} of each in orange and FBP
in blue. The orange stretches meet down the middle, where one pair of subunits
holds onto the other, and FBP binds where each stretch ends, at residues
432–437. UniProt annotates residues {swapStart}–{swapEnd} as an intersubunit
contact.

The gene page's own partner interface comes from a different model: AlphaFold DB
predicts PKM only as a dimer, and most of that pair's interface lies outside the
stretch. Of its {contacts.length} interface residues, {inSwap.length} fall in
the swapped stretch.

## Where M1's exon is included

In the gene's full exon numbering, M1's exon is exon 9 and M2's is exon 10. Each
is joined to exon 8 upstream and exon 11 downstream, so a read across any of
those four junctions shows which exon its molecule carried. The sashimi plot
draws all four, with M2's above the exons and M1's below, in five GTEx tissues
and a chronic myeloid leukemia cell line.

In skeletal muscle the M1 arcs carry 1.6 and 2.1 million reads and the M2 arcs
under 100,000 each; in liver and the leukemia cell line it is the other way
round. The reads into M2's exon and out of it agree within 10% in every row,
while M1's arc out of its exon has a fifth to a half fewer reads than its arc
in, which we have not looked into.

Counting only the junctions into exon 11, the reads from M1's exon make up{' '}
{tissueShare(e, 'Muscle - Skeletal')} of those in skeletal muscle,{' '}
{tissueShare(e, 'Brain - Cerebellar Hemisphere')} in cerebellar hemisphere and{' '}
{tissueShare(e, 'Heart - Left Ventricle')} in heart left ventricle, against{' '}
{tissueShare(e, 'Liver')} in liver and {tissueShare(e, 'Whole Blood')}
in whole blood. M2 is the form proliferating and tumour cells use
([Christofk et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06734)).

GTEx's transcript TPM gives M1's transcripts at most {largestTpmShare(e)} of the
gene in any tissue, skeletal muscle included. NDUFC2 isoform 4 failed the other
way, rare by its junction and a third of the gene by TPM;
[Exploring tissue-specific splicing](../2026-11-16-where-isoforms-are-made/) explains the
junction count.
