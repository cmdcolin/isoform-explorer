Isoform explorer compares an isoform's sequence with the canonical protein's.
To find where either is made, you need RNA or protein data from elsewhere, and
the easiest numbers to find, GTEx's per-transcript expression, can be wrong for
an isoform. This post compares them with a direct count of reads on two
isoforms, then lists public sources with data for each isoform.

## Two ways to measure an isoform's share

GTEx v8 sequenced bulk RNA from 54 adult tissues. For each GENCODE transcript it
reports TPM, which RSEM estimates by assigning each short read to the
transcripts it could have come from. When the only sequence unique to an isoform
is one splice junction, RSEM has little to go on.

The direct count is the reads that cross that junction. For each isoform below,
we took the splice junction its transcript has and the canonical's lacks, and
the canonical junction that starts or ends at the same splice site. The junction
share is the isoform's reads as a fraction of both. We counted reads in
recount's realignment of the GTEx v8 samples through
[Snaptron](https://snaptron.cs.jhu.edu/), grouped by tissue. The counts are our own tally of public
data on {FETCHED}, and `pnpm expression` in the repository reproduces it.

In the chart above, blue circles are the junction share and orange diamonds the
TPM share. The sashimi plots below draw those junctions, and the canonical's
other junctions between the exons shown, as arcs labelled with the reads that
cross them, in three of the tissues.

## APP695: highest in brain by TPM and by junction reads

APP770, the canonical amyloid precursor protein, has a Kunitz protease inhibitor
domain encoded by exon 7 and an OX-2 motif encoded by exon 8. Variants in APP
cause an inherited form of Alzheimer disease
(<a href={alzheimer ? diseaseHref(alzheimer) : geneHref('APP')}>{alzheimer?.name}</a>
in UniProt).

<a href={geneHref('APP', 'P05067-4')}>APP695 skips both</a>. The junction share
of APP695 is {tissueShare(app, 'Brain - Cerebellar Hemisphere')} in cerebellar
hemisphere and {tissueShare(app, 'Brain - Frontal Cortex (BA9)')} in frontal
cortex, against {tissueShare(app, 'Liver')} in liver, and the TPM share follows
it.

In brain, the blue arc that jumps from exon 6 to exon 9 is the thicker one; in
liver, nearly every read goes from exon 6 into exon 7.

Studies from before RNA-seq found the same. In 1988 the Kunitz exon was found in
cDNAs from tissues outside the brain
([Tanzi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/331528a0)). Ribonuclease protection in
human tissues then put APP695 mRNA ahead of the Kunitz forms in cerebral cortex
([Tanaka et al., 1989](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0006-291x(89)92760-5>)), and PCR
found it predominant in brain and minor elsewhere, in rat and human
([Kang and Müller-Hill, 1990](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0006-291x(90)90992-v>)).
In rat brain cell cultures, APP695 mRNA was highest in neurons, and astrocytes
had about three times more Kunitz-containing APP protein
([Rohan de Silva et al., 1997](<https://doi.org/10.1016/s0169-328x(97)00045-4>)).

## NDUFC2 isoform 4: a warning about TPM

NDUFC2 is not a case of tissue-specific splicing. It is here because GTEx
transcript TPM gives isoform 4 over a third of the gene in every tissue, the
orange diamonds lined up near 40% in the chart above, while the reads say the
isoform is hardly made anywhere.

<a href={geneHref('NDUFC2', 'O95298-2')}>NDUFC2 isoform 4</a> lacks the
protein's only transmembrane helix, through a splice acceptor 69 bases inside
exon 2. TPM gives it more of the gene than the canonical transcript, but the
isoform's junction has
{ndufc2.isoformReads} reads in {ndufc2.isoformSamples} of
{ndufc2.samples.toLocaleString()} samples, and the canonical junction from the
same splice site has {ndufc2.canonicalReads.toLocaleString()}. One ENCODE
long-read sample, embryonic kidney
([ENCFF529HGR](https://www.encodeproject.org/files/ENCFF529HGR/)), has 405
full-length reads of the canonical transcript and none of isoform 4's.

We have not traced why RSEM credits the isoform so heavily. Its transcript
differs from the canonical in that one junction and in a shorter 3′ end, so
reads in the coding exons fit both, and the shorter transcript may absorb them.
Whatever the cause, a TPM table alone would have made a rarely seen isoform look
like the major form.

## The worked examples

The other worked examples each get the same check:

- [ZDHHC16 isoform 3](../2026-11-16-zdhhc16-active-site/), which lacks the
  catalytic cysteine, has {share(zdhhc16)} of junction reads overall and{' '}
  {tissueShare(zdhhc16, 'Muscle - Skeletal')} in skeletal muscle, and TPM
  agrees.
- [PAR3 isoform 2](../2026-11-16-par3-signal-peptide/), which starts after the
  signal peptide, has {share(f2rl2)} of junction reads, though RSEM gives it 0
  TPM in every tissue.
- [NDUFC2 isoform 4](../2026-11-16-ndufc2-membrane-helix/) is the case above.
- [Pyruvate kinase M1](../2026-11-16-pkm-subunit-contact/) fails the other way:
  {tissueShare(pkm, 'Muscle - Skeletal')} of junction reads in skeletal muscle,
  and never more than {largestTpmShare(pkm)} of the gene by TPM.
- [CEBPB LIP](../2026-11-16-cebpb-start-codons/) has no junction to check: it
  is made from a later start codon on the same mRNA as the other two forms.

## Public data on isoform expression

- **Transcript expression by tissue:** [GTEx](https://gtexportal.org/) has an
  API (`medianTranscriptExpression`) and bulk files, under GTEx's terms of use.
  The [Human Protein Atlas](https://www.proteinatlas.org/) shows RNA by isoform
  on each gene's Isoform tab and offers a transcript download under CC BY 4.0.
- **Whether a splice is made at all:** [Snaptron](https://snaptron.cs.jhu.edu/)
  and [recount3](https://rna.recount.bio/) give junction counts for GTEx, TCGA
  and public SRA samples.
- **Exon inclusion by tissue:** [VastDB](https://vastdb.crg.eu/) and
  [ASCOT](http://ascot.cs.jhu.edu/) report percent spliced in for exons and
  splice sites across tissues and cell types.
- **Full-length molecules:** [ENCODE](https://www.encodeproject.org/) long-read
  RNA-seq quantifies transcripts in over 300 samples with no reuse restrictions;
  GTEx's long-read set
  ([Glinos et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35922509/)) covers 88
  samples.
- **Single cells:** isoform-level single-cell data are mostly brain studies. A
  long-read single-cell atlas across 26 tissues
  ([Mantri et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596200/)) is
  a preprint, with data under CC BY 4.0.
- **Protein:** [PeptideAtlas](https://peptideatlas.org/) maps peptides to
  UniProt isoform accessions. A peptide found in only one isoform is evidence
  for that isoform, and you have to filter for those yourself. ZDHHC16 isoform
  3, PAR3 isoform 2 and NDUFC2 isoform 4 have none.
