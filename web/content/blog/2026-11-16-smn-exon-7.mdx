## Two genes, one base apart in exon 7

The human genome has two nearly identical genes side by side, _SMN1_ and _SMN2_ ([Lefebvre et al., 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(95)90460-3)).
One base differs in exon 7, and with the _SMN2_ base most transcripts skip
that exon ([Lorson et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.96.11.6307)).
People with spinal muscular atrophy lack a working _SMN1_, so their protein
comes from _SMN2_, and according to UniProt the severity of the disease depends
mainly on how many copies of _SMN2_ they have.

## A drug that changes splicing

Antisense oligonucleotides are short, modified strands of nucleic acid that
bind a matching stretch of RNA. Ones that cover a splicing silencer in the
intron after exon 7 made _SMN2_ keep the exon in mice
([Hua et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2008.01.014)). One such
drug, nusinersen, was tested in infants with spinal muscular atrophy: more of
the infants given it reached motor milestones and survived without permanent
ventilation than of those given a sham procedure
([Finkel et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1702752)).

## RNA-seq reads fit SMN1 and SMN2

_SMN1_ and _SMN2_ differ at only a few bases, so an RNA-seq read that covers
none of them could come from either gene. Reads that skip exon 7 come from both
genes' transcripts, and only those that cover one of the differing bases can be
told apart.
