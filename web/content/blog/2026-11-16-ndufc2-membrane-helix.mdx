NDUFC2 is a {s.record.length}-residue subunit of complex I, the first enzyme of
the mitochondrial respiratory chain. UniProt annotates one transmembrane helix
in it, residues 56–75. The gene page sums up isoform {s.isoform.accession}
(UniProt's isoform {s.isoform.name}) in one sentence:

> {s.answer}

## Exon 2 without its first 69 bases

The picture above is the gene page's Gene + protein view, with the helix
highlighted. Its top half draws one GENCODE transcript per isoform. The
canonical transcript, {canonical}, and isoform 4's,{' '}
{s.isoform.transcripts.ensembl[0]}, both have three exons, but exon 2 of isoform
4's begins 69 bases later, at a second splice acceptor inside the canonical
exon.

Since 69 bases are 23 codons, the reading frame holds. The protein lacks
residues 56–78 and continues from residue 79 to the canonical end. On the
protein map alone the gap could pass for a skipped exon; the gene view shows
that all three exons are there and the second one is shorter.

The view's lower half is the protein map. Exon 1 encodes residues 1 to 55, so
the gap starts where exon 1 ends, and it covers the helix from end to end. The
other alternative isoform, O95298-3, keeps the helix and differs from residue 77
on.

Without the helix, isoform 4 has no annotated segment that spans a membrane.
Whether it is made as a protein or joins complex I is outside what the
annotation shows.

## How often isoform 4 is made

Rarely, if at all, by the evidence we found. No paper studies it, and its RefSeq
transcript rests on one EST from an ovarian adenocarcinoma cell line
([BE740715.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/BE740715.1)). In GTEx v8, the
junction that defines it has {e.isoformReads} reads in {e.isoformSamples} of{' '}
{e.samples.toLocaleString()} samples, against{' '}
{e.canonicalReads.toLocaleString()} for the canonical junction from the same
splice site. No peptide unique to it has been reported.

GTEx's transcript TPM says the opposite, crediting isoform 4 with over a third
of the gene in every tissue.
